𝐎𝐛𝐣𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐯𝐞: To design, simulate, and validate a Convergent-Divergent rocket nozzle for a small turbopump-fed liquid rocket engine, benchmarking RPA analytical predictions against high-fidelity CFD and coupled thermal-structural FEA.

𝐀𝐩𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐚𝐜𝐡 & 𝐌𝐞𝐭𝐡𝐨𝐝𝐨𝐥𝐨𝐠𝐲: Designed a parametric C-D nozzle in Autodesk Fusion 360 based on analytical design principles, predicting thrust, specific impulse (Isp), and exit velocity using Rocket Propulsion Analysis (RPA) software.

Performed 2D axisymmetric, transient, pressure-based CFD in ANSYS Fluent using the k-ω SST turbulence model to study compressible supersonic gas dynamics, capturing choked flow at the throat, supersonic expansion, shock structures, and overexpanded exhaust plume behaviour at exit.

Conducted coupled thermal-structural FEA of an Inconel 718 nozzle in Ansys Mechanical to evaluate stress, deformation, factor of safety, heat flux, and temperature under combustion conditions.

Validated CFD results against RPA thermodynamic predictions, achieving close agreement in bulk parameters: mass flow rate (within 3%), exhaust velocity (within 6%), and Isp (within 9%). Local exit-plane parameters showed larger deviations due to RPA ideal 1D assumptions agianst CFD resolved 2D viscous flow.

𝐋𝐞𝐚𝐫𝐧𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐎𝐮𝐭𝐜𝐨𝐦𝐞: This project deepened my understanding of high-speed gas dynamics and how theory translates into real nozzle behaviour. Correlations like the Bartz equation and Chapman-Enskog kinetic theory gave me a real feel for how nozzles scale in practice.

Building the full CFD-to-FEA workflow myself including meshing, resolving shocks, then feeding results into a structural model showed how different physics domains connect in a real propulsion system, and what structural safety means under Inconel 718 combustion loads.

This was my first end-to-end project, from design through CFD into FEA, and it taught me patience and the discipline of cross-checking every result before trusting it.
